# Body shopping versus agent shopping

*Part three of three in the Recipes from the Softbake kitchen series. Category Delivery model. Status coming soon.*

> **Status.** This recipe is still proofing. The outline below is the promise. The full article will land in this folder as `README.md` once it is ready to taste.

## Why this recipe matters

Most agencies still sell software the same way they did fifteen years ago. A client describes a problem, the agency assembles a team of developer bodies, and the invoice grows by the hour until either the budget or the patience runs out. The industry name for this model is body shopping, and even the people who profit from it know it rewards time in the chair more than cake on the plate.

Code agents change the maths underneath that model. A small kitchen with one master chief and a handful of agents can now ship the kind of product that used to need a team of six. The question is no longer whether agents can do the work. It is how to sell, plan, and price it without dragging the old habits along for the ride. We call the alternative agent shopping, and this article is the honest comparison between the two.

## What the article will cover

It will lay out both models side by side. Body shopping at its best, with senior people, real reviews, and a clean handover, and agent shopping at its best, with a master chief, a prepared kitchen, and outcomes priced as deliverables rather than as hours.

From there the piece will go through the dimensions that actually matter to a client. Cost, with a worked comparison on a small product. Speed, with realistic expectations rather than agency theatre. Accountability, including who answers the phone when something breaks at midnight. Quality, including the human checks that keep agent code from earning a bad name. And what happens when the project ends, which is where most engagements quietly leak value.

It will also cover the parts of agent shopping that nobody puts in the sales deck. The kinds of work agents are still bad at, the rituals you cannot cut without paying for them later, and the contracts and pricing structures that keep both sides honest when one party is suddenly five times faster than the other.

## What you will take away

A clear, fair model for choosing between body shopping and agent shopping for any given project, with the questions to ask a supplier on either side and the red flags that mean you are about to pay a body shopping invoice for work an agent kitchen quietly produced.

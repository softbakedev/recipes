# Planning a sprint kitchen for baker agents

*Part two of three in the Recipes from the Softbake kitchen series. Category Delivery. Status coming soon.*

> **Status.** This recipe is still proofing. The outline below is the promise. The full article will land in this folder as `README.md` once it is ready to taste.

## Why this recipe is coming next

The first recipe explained how to turn an idea into something a kitchen can actually bake. The natural follow up is the question every team asks once the recipe card is on the wall. How do we plan a sprint when the bakers are agents and a job that used to take two days finishes in twenty minutes.

Most planning rituals were designed for human throughput. Two week sprints, story points, velocity charts, standups timed to morning coffee. Drop agents into that kitchen and the ceremony starts to feel slow. The board is stale by lunchtime, the burndown chart looks like a cliff, and nobody is sure whether they finished early or never planned enough work in the first place.

This article will walk through how we re-shape planning around agent throughput without losing the human checks that keep quality honest.

## What the article will cover

It will start with how to slice a recipe card into bake sized tasks an agent can pick up without a meeting, with the same definition of fresh discipline introduced in the first recipe. It will then look at how parallel agents are sequenced, when a single branch is enough and when it is worth splitting work across worktrees so two agents can bake in parallel without stepping on the same dough.

From there the piece will tackle the harder questions. How long is a sprint when the unit of work takes minutes instead of days. How do you write a plan that survives an agent finishing a week of tickets before lunch. What does a daily kitchen check in look like when most of the work happens between human reviews rather than during them. And how do you keep the timeline honest with a client when speed becomes the easiest part of the job.

The closing section will offer a small planning template the Softbake team uses in real projects, with the rituals that earned their keep, the ones that quietly disappeared once agents joined the kitchen, and the one number we still report at the end of every sprint.

## What you will take away

A practical way to plan agent led work without copying scrum, kanban, or shape up wholesale, written for teams that have one master chief, a handful of agents, and a real client waiting for a working product.
